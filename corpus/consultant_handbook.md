# Pine Harbor Analytics | Consultant Handbook

> Fictional training document — not an actual employer policy.


## 1. First-week onboarding checklist


### Before the first day

The staffing coordinator sends a welcome email with the project name, manager contact, expected start date, work location, and a link to the approved onboarding portal. If any item is missing, reply to the coordinator before the start date. Do not send passwords or identity documents by ordinary email.


### Day 1

Attend the virtual or in-person orientation scheduled by the project coordinator. Confirm that you can access the company email, collaboration platform, time-entry portal, and required project workspace. Review the security and acceptable-use materials assigned to you.


### Days 1–2

Meet your project manager and confirm your role, deliverables, communication channels, meeting schedule, and escalation path. Ask which project code and cost center to use for time entry. If a code is not provided, do not guess; contact the project manager.


### Days 2–3

Complete assigned security awareness and data-handling training. Use only approved devices and storage locations for company or client information. Report a suspected phishing message or accidental disclosure to the security contact promptly.


### Days 3–5

Confirm that you can submit a test or draft timesheet if your manager requests one. Review the expense submission process before incurring reimbursable costs. Confirm whether your assignment requires onsite attendance or permits remote work.


### End of week

Have a short check-in with your project manager. Review access issues, open onboarding tasks, deliverables, and questions. The company does not specify a universal first-week completion bonus or a fixed onboarding duration.


## 1. First-week checklist (continued)


### Access troubleshooting

If access fails, capture the error message without including passwords, authentication codes, or sensitive client data. Send the screenshot through the approved support channel. Include your name, project, system name, approximate time, and a short description.


### Identity and payroll forms

Complete any required onboarding forms through the secure portal provided by the employer. This fictional handbook does not collect Social Security numbers, bank account details, tax forms, or immigration documents. Use the employer's approved secure workflow.


### Project readiness

Before handling production data, verify that your manager has approved access and that you have completed required training. Use synthetic or approved test data for practice. Never copy production data to a personal device or personal cloud account.

□ Welcome email reviewed. □ Manager and project contacts confirmed. □ Required accounts tested. □ Security training completed. □ Time-entry project code confirmed. □ Remote/onsite expectations confirmed. □ Open questions escalated.


### What is not specified

The handbook does not specify a mandatory number of orientation hours, a universal equipment delivery date, or a fixed deadline for all onboarding forms. Ask the coordinator for assignment-specific instructions.


## 2. Timesheets and payroll


### Time-entry system

Submit time through the fictional Pine Harbor Time Portal. The portal address is provided individually by the staffing coordinator and is not reproduced in this training document. Use your assigned account; never share credentials.


### Recording time

Enter the actual hours worked for each workday and the correct project code. Record time promptly and accurately. Do not round, inflate, copy another person's entry, or enter hours for work that was not performed. If you make an error, use the portal's correction workflow or contact the time administrator.


### Submission deadline

The fictional handbook asks consultants to submit a complete timesheet by 5:00 p.m. local time each Friday. This is a sample company deadline only. If a holiday or portal outage affects submission, notify your manager and time administrator before the deadline when possible.


### Manager approval

The project manager reviews submitted time. A timesheet may be returned for clarification if the project code, dates, hours, or description are incomplete. Respond through the portal or approved company channel. Do not alter a previously approved timesheet without following the correction process.


### Payroll questions

For a missing payment, incorrect hours, or portal issue, contact payroll-support@pineharbor.example (fictional). Include the pay period and a brief description. Do not include full bank account numbers or tax identification numbers in an ordinary email.


## 2. Timesheets and payroll (continued)


### Pay schedule

The exact pay frequency, pay dates, pay-period boundaries, overtime approval process, and payroll cutoff exceptions are Not specified in source. Ask fictional Payroll Support or your staffing coordinator. The assistant must not infer a pay date from the Friday timesheet deadline.


### Corrections and records

Keep your own accurate record of hours worked and retain confirmation of submissions in accordance with your employer's instructions. If you believe recorded time is inaccurate, raise the issue promptly with your manager and payroll support.


### Tax forms

Tax forms are handled through the employer's secure payroll workflow. This handbook does not explain how to complete Form W-4, how much tax will be withheld, or how to file a tax return. Consult official IRS resources or a qualified tax professional for general tax questions.


### W-2 questions

The fictional company provides year-end tax documents through its secure payroll portal when applicable. The timing, delivery method, and correction procedure for a specific individual's tax form are Not specified in source. Contact Payroll Support for account-specific help.


### Important boundary

The assistant may summarize general public information about wage records or tax forms, but it must not calculate an individual's tax, decide whether someone is an employee or contractor, or promise a particular pay amount.


## 3. PTO, holidays, and leave


### Requesting planned time away

Submit a planned time-away request to your project manager using the fictional scheduling workflow and notify the project team about coverage. The handbook does not publish a PTO accrual rate, annual PTO bank, waiting period, carryover limit, or approval guarantee.


### Company holidays

The fictional company observes holidays according to the project calendar distributed by the project manager. The list of paid holidays, whether a holiday is paid for every consultant, and holiday premium rules are Not specified in source. Confirm the applicable client and payroll calendar.


### Unexpected absence

If illness, emergency, or another unexpected event prevents you from working, notify your project manager as soon as reasonably possible using the agreed contact channel. If you cannot reach the manager, contact the staffing coordinator.


### Leave documentation

Do not send detailed medical information to the project team unless an approved process specifically requests it. Contact HR through the secure channel for confidential leave questions. This fictional handbook does not define medical certification requirements or statutory leave eligibility.


### Coverage and handoff

For planned absences, document active tasks, deadlines, key contacts, and any approved handoff. Do not share credentials with a substitute. Ask your manager to arrange access through the standard access-control process.


## 3. PTO, holidays, and leave (continued)


### Family or medical leave questions

The handbook does not decide whether a consultant or employee qualifies for FMLA or another leave program. Eligibility can depend on employer coverage, work history, hours, location, and other facts. Refer general questions to official Department of Labor information and personal eligibility questions to HR or a qualified professional.


### Military, jury, and other leave

The fictional company does not specify separate policies for military leave, jury duty, bereavement, voting, or other protected absences in this handbook. Contact HR before assuming a particular paid or unpaid entitlement.


### No automated eligibility decisions

The assistant must not approve or deny leave, calculate entitlement, interpret medical records, or make legal determinations. It should explain that the approved documents do not establish the person's eligibility and direct the person to HR.


### Not specified in source

PTO accrual, paid sick leave, unpaid leave, holiday pay, carryover, blackout dates, required notice periods, and approval authority beyond the project manager are Not specified in source.


### Helpful question to HR

When contacting HR, ask which policy applies to your assignment, what secure form or portal to use, what notice is expected, and who can explain confidential eligibility questions.


## 4. Equipment and remote work


### Company equipment

Use company-issued equipment for company work when provided. Keep laptops, security keys, monitors, and other assigned equipment physically secure. Do not lend equipment to family members or other people.


### Device security

Use a strong device password, enable screen locking, install approved updates, and use company-approved endpoint protection. Do not disable security controls. Report a lost or stolen device immediately to the service desk and your manager.


### Remote-work environment

Work from a reasonably private location and prevent unauthorized people from viewing confidential information. Use headphones for sensitive calls where appropriate. Avoid public Wi-Fi unless using the company-approved secure connection.


### Data handling

Store work files only in approved company or client repositories. Do not forward work files to personal email, upload them to personal storage, or paste confidential content into unapproved AI tools. Follow the stricter client rule when project requirements differ.


### Remote-work approval

Whether a particular assignment is remote, hybrid, or onsite is determined by the project assignment and manager. This handbook does not promise remote-work eligibility or reimbursement for home internet, furniture, or utilities.


## 4. Equipment and remote work (continued)


### Software and access

Install software only from approved company sources or with IT authorization. Request access using the service desk workflow and state the business reason. Access is granted according to role and project need.


### Meetings and availability

Follow the meeting schedule and availability expectations agreed with your project manager. If connectivity fails, notify the team through an alternate approved channel when possible.


### Ergonomics and safety

Arrange your workspace to support safe and comfortable work. Report equipment defects or safety concerns to the service desk or manager. This handbook does not provide a legal assessment of a home office or workplace.


### Return of equipment

When an assignment ends or equipment is replaced, follow the return instructions issued by IT or the staffing coordinator. Return all company property and confirm receipt using the approved process. The return shipping method and deadline are provided case by case.


### Not specified in source

The handbook does not specify equipment purchase allowances, home-office stipends, internet reimbursements, replacement fees, or a universal equipment-return deadline.


## 5. Expenses and reimbursements


### Approval before spending

Obtain written manager approval before incurring a project expense. Approval should identify the business purpose, expected amount, project, and any spending limit. A manager's informal comment should not be treated as unlimited approval.


### Submitting an expense

Use the fictional Expense Portal. Provide the transaction date, vendor, amount, currency, business purpose, project code, and itemized receipt when available. Submit expenses within 30 calendar days of the transaction in this fictional workflow.


### Receipts and records

Keep itemized receipts and any required business-purpose notes. If a receipt is unavailable, ask the expense administrator whether an exception form is permitted. Do not alter a receipt or split a transaction to avoid an approval limit.


### Travel expenses

Travel must be approved in advance. The handbook does not establish airfare class, hotel caps, meal per diem, mileage rate, rental-car rules, or cancellation policy. Obtain assignment-specific approval before booking.


### Non-reimbursable examples

Personal purchases, commuting costs, unapproved upgrades, expenses for family members, and expenses without a documented business purpose are not automatically reimbursable. Final decisions follow the actual company policy and applicable law.


## 5. Expenses and reimbursements (continued)


### Review and payment

The expense administrator checks submissions for completeness and manager approval. A submission may be returned for missing receipts or unclear business purpose. Reimbursement timing and payment method are Not specified in source.


### Tax questions

Whether a payment or reimbursement is taxable depends on applicable rules and the facts. This handbook does not determine tax treatment. Consult official IRS guidance or a qualified tax professional.


### Expense privacy

Do not include full payment-card numbers, bank credentials, or sensitive personal data in expense notes. Use the secure portal and follow the company's retention rules.


### Disputed expense

If an expense is denied, review the reason in the portal and contact the expense administrator with supporting documentation. Escalate unresolved policy questions to HR or Finance.


### Public reference boundary

IRS Publication 463 is a general tax reference about travel, gift, and car expenses. It does not replace Pine Harbor's fictional approval rules and does not guarantee that a consultant's expense will be reimbursed.
